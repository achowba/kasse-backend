# Language and style convention

How prose is written in this repository: READMEs, TSDoc, comments, commit messages, and pull request descriptions.

## Be direct

- Say what a thing does. Do not say how good it is.
- One idea per sentence. Short sentences.
- Active voice. "The filter rejects the request", not "the request is rejected by the filter".
- Present tense for behaviour. "Returns `null` when the plan is zero", not "will return".
- Lead with the point. The reason can follow it.

## Do not

- Do not use filler: "simply", "just", "basically", "of course", "note that", "it should be noted".
- Do not use marketing words: "robust", "seamless", "powerful", "blazing fast", "world class", "enterprise grade", "best practice".
- Do not praise the code. "A clean, elegant solution" tells a reader nothing. Describe what it does and what it costs.
- Do not hedge a fact. If it is true, state it. If it is uncertain, say what is uncertain and why.
- Do not repeat a rule that has a home elsewhere. Link to it.

## Punctuation

- No em dashes, no en dashes, and no double hyphens in prose. Use a comma, a colon, or a full stop. A hyphen inside a compound word is fine.
- Use a colon to introduce a list or an explanation.
- Spell out a range as "6 to 8", not with a dash.

## Precision

- Name the thing. "The `plans` collection", not "the data".
- Give the number. "Under roughly 400 lines", not "small".
- Define an acronym on first use in a document.
- When describing a tradeoff, state both sides and which was chosen.

## Structure

- A document opens with what it is for.
- Use a table when comparing more than two things across the same fields.
- Use a heading a reader can scan to. Do not bury a rule in a paragraph.
